# Why HR is getting security keys

> Plain-language explainer for HR staff. Fictional company.

You already use a second step when you sign in. You type your password, then you tap
"Approve" on your phone. That step is called multi-factor authentication, or MFA. It stops most
attacks. But it does not stop all of them.

## The trick that gets past it

Some phishing emails now send you to a fake sign-in page that looks exactly like ours. Here is
what happens:

1. You type your password on the fake page.
2. The fake page passes it to the real site right away.
3. The real site asks for your approval, and your phone buzzes.
4. You tap "Approve," because you really are signing in.
5. The attacker is now signed in as you.

Your phone cannot tell the real page from the fake one. So the approval goes through.

## Why this matters for HR

HR accounts can see pay, tax, and benefits records for all of our employees. One stolen HR
sign-in could expose about 1,200 people's personal data. That is the highest risk we found
in this year's review.

## How a security key fixes it

A security key is a small device that plugs into your laptop or taps your phone. It checks the
web address before it answers. On a fake page, it simply does not respond. You do not have to
spot the fake. The key does it for you.

## What changes for you

- You will get a key in early December.
- When you sign in to the HR Portal, you touch the key instead of tapping "Approve."
- Keep it on your keyring. If you lose it, call the help desk the same day.

Setup takes about ten minutes. Each key costs about $50.
