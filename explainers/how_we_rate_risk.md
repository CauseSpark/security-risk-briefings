# How we rate security risk

> Plain-language explainer for managers. Fictional company.

We rate every risk by asking two questions. Then we combine the answers.

## Question 1: How likely is it?

This has two parts. First, how likely is someone or something to try? Phishing emails arrive
every day, so that part is very likely. A flood at our cloud provider's site is very rare.

Second, if it happens, how likely is it to do harm? Good defenses lower this part. A locked
door does not stop people from trying the handle. It stops the try from working.

## Question 2: How bad would it be?

We look at what would be lost or broken. For the HR Portal, the worst case is employee
personal data getting out. That brings legal duties, costs, and a loss of trust. A short outage
is less serious, because HR can use paper forms for a few days.

## Putting them together

We use a table from a federal guide, NIST SP 800-30. It turns the two answers into one of five
levels: Very Low, Low, Moderate, High, or Very High.

| | Low harm | Moderate harm | High harm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Likely** | Low | Moderate | High |
| **Possible** | Low | Moderate | Moderate |
| **Unlikely** | Low | Low | Low |

*(A simplified view. The real table has five rows and five columns.)*

## What we do with the rating

High risks get fixed first, within 30 days. Moderate risks get a plan with dates. Low risks are
often accepted, which means we watch them but do not spend money on them yet.

This year we rated 15 risks for the HR Portal. One was High. Five were Moderate. The rest were
Low or Very Low.
