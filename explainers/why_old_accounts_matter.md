# Why old accounts are a problem

> Plain-language explainer for IT and HR. Fictional company.

An account nobody uses is like a spare key under the mat. You forget it is there. Someone else
might not.

## What we found

Our review found six accounts on the HR Portal's servers that no one had used in 41 to 97
days. They were still turned on. One belonged to a contractor who had left in July. Others
were set up for one-time jobs and never cleaned up.

## Why it matters

- **No one is watching them.** If someone signs in with one, nobody expects that activity, so
  nobody notices.
- **Passwords leak.** Old passwords show up in breaches of other sites. If an old account still
  works, a leaked password is all it takes.
- **Some have strong access.** Several of these accounts could reach the database that holds
  employee records.

## What we are changing

Our rule already said to turn off accounts after 35 days without use. The problem was that a
person had to check by hand each month, and the check missed these accounts. Now a script will
check every day and turn them off on its own. The security officer gets an alert for anything
it turns off.

## What you can do

- When someone leaves your team, tell HR the same day.
- If you set up an account for a short job, put an end date on the request.
- If you get an alert that your account was turned off, call the help desk. It is easy to turn
  back on.
