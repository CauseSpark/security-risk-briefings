# What is a POA&M?

> Plain-language explainer for anyone at Acme. Fictional company.

POA&M stands for **Plan of Action and Milestones**. It is a to-do list for security fixes.

## Why we keep one

Every security review finds things to fix. Some fixes take an hour. Others need money, a
vendor, or a few months. The POA&M makes sure none of them get lost.

## What each item says

Each item answers six questions:

1. **What is wrong?** In plain words, like "old accounts are not turned off."
2. **How serious is it?** High, Moderate, or Low.
3. **Who owns the fix?** One named person, not a team.
4. **What are the steps?** These are the milestones, and each one has a date.
5. **What will it cost?** Hours of work or dollars.
6. **When will it be done?** Our policy sets a limit. High items get 30 days. Moderate items get
   90 days. Low items get 180 days.

## How an item ends

An item ends in one of two ways.

- **Fixed.** Someone shows proof that the fix works, like a new scan. The security officer
  checks the proof before closing it.
- **Risk accepted.** Sometimes a fix is not possible. The CIO can sign off on living with the
  risk, but only for one year. Then it must be looked at again.

## What you might be asked

If you own an item, you will get a reminder before each milestone is due. If a date will slip,
say so early. A late item with a new plan is much better than a late item nobody mentioned.
